Taste decides what gets saved

Who cannot be on at once

Each act asks Qloo to rank every other act on the bill. Acts that rank each other high share a crowd, even across genres, comedy and podcasts.

Which set to lose

When a set must go, Qloo popularity says which. Without it, the same solver can drop a far bigger name.

Why, in words you can check

Qloo gives the tastes two crowds share. You can see the reason and overrule it.

Fair questions

What can the agent not do?

It cannot make up a schedule: only the solver produces one, and a separate validator rechecks it. Every number in its summary must exist in the tool results. It cannot approve or send anything.

How do you know Qloo is right about crowds?

We don't, not fully. In a test set in advance, the clashes the press named at Outside Lands 2026 did not stand out overall: 3 of 8 testable pairs sit in the stronger half, though The Strokes × The xx ranked first. We have not measured attendance. The evaluation has the details.

Are the days real?

The set times are published ones, and the Qloo rows are real. The problems on Outside Lands and Lollapalooza are examples, and stage sizes and artist hours are assumed. The Bonnaroo storm happened. All four days play a saved run until you change something, and the page tells you which you are watching.

What happens to my lineup and the API keys?

The Qloo and Gemini keys stay on the server, and every visitor has hourly limits. Act names go to Qloo and your messages go to Google's Gemini API on a free-tier key, so do not paste anything confidential. This free server can restart when idle. Your browser keeps a copy of your own lineup.

Can another agent use it?

Yes. Counterset is also an MCP server at /mcp, with tools to list days, audit a day, rescue a day and explain a pair.
