First it reads the message

A programmer pastes what production said. The agent records each closure and limit, and every one must quote the message word for word.

    Taste decides what gets saved

    Every set counts the same to a solver. Qloo is what tells it who the sets are for, and it does three jobs here that the solver and the model cannot.

    Who cannot be on at once

    Each act asks Qloo to rank every other act on the bill, comedians and podcasts included. Two acts that rank each other near the top share a crowd, so they should not play against each other.

    Which set to lose

    When a set must go, the smallest draw goes first, using Qloo popularity. Without it the same solver drops whichever act its search reaches, and that can be a far bigger name.

    Why, in words a person can check

    Qloo's audience comparison gives the taste tags two crowds share. A programmer can see the reason and overrule it, or mark a clash as intentional.

    Models never invent a pair. An act Qloo cannot identify stays unknown, never zero: 11 of the 28 acts here, because a wrong match would mean a wrong answer.

    Fair questions

    Did Bonnaroo get it wrong?

    We do not claim that. The replay assumes every act was still there and willing to play less, and the people on site knew about crew, power, mud and flights. Our reconstruction of the day they ran breaks none of the rules we model, which checks our rules, not their decisions. The point is that the options can be seen in minutes, not that one choice was wrong.

    What is the agent, and what can't it do?

    A model reads the message and chooses tools: close a stage, audit the grid, try a rescue under stated terms, compare without Qloo. It cannot make up a schedule, because only the solver produces one, and a separate validator rechecks the result. Every number in its summary must exist in the tool results. It cannot approve anything: a person does.

    Does it change who plays?

    Only if you let it. Moving sets is the default, and shortening or dropping them are terms a person turns on. Headliners you mark are never shortened or dropped. Every affected act gets a drafted message, for a person to send.

    Is this only for storms?

    No. The same desk audits a timetable before it is published, and handles a late flight or a single stage closing. On the published Lollapalooza 2024 Saturday it finds three shared-crowd clashes, and it can move three acts to remove them. It also works across kinds of act: on a rehearsal bill of music, comedy and podcasts, 14 of 31 pairs crossed kinds.

    How do you know Qloo is right about crowds?

    We don't, not fully. On Lollapalooza, three of ListenBrainz's four reciprocal pairs were also Qloo pairs, and Qloo ranked 496 pairs where that data links 11. Genre tags mostly disagree with it. In a test we set in advance, the clashes the press named at Outside Lands 2026 did not stand out on Qloo: 3 of 8 testable pairs sit in the stronger half, in a small sample. We have not measured attendance or asked a programmer to compare blind, and we say so in the evaluation.

    Can another agent use it?

    Yes. Counterset is also an MCP server at /mcp, with tools to list days, audit a day, rescue a day and explain a pair. They read recorded Qloo rows and change nothing.
