## Why Qloo rather than an LLM, genre tags or public similar-artist data

- **Coverage of exactly this bill.** One request per act and kind ranks every other act of that kind on the bill. On Lollapalooza 2024 Saturday that gave 496 of 861 pairs ranked in both directions (the rest involve the 10 acts left unresolved). ListenBrainz's public similar-artist lists (top 100 per act) linked 11 of the 861 pairs, even though 27 acts have lists.
- **Across kinds of act.** On the multi-arts rehearsal bill, 14 of 31 cues connect a musician, comedian or podcast to a different kind. Genre tags and music co-listening data cannot rank a comedian against a band.
- **Agreement where the open data does speak.** Three of ListenBrainz's four reciprocal pairs on the Lollapalooza bill are also Qloo cues. Genre tags overlap little (4 of 17 genre pairs are Qloo cues), because they pair by label, not audience.
- **An LLM can invent associations.** In the comparison, the LLM-only schedule passed every hard rule but ended exactly where the no-evidence solver did; it cannot show provenance for a pair, and the app never lets a model supply evidence.

What this does **not** establish: that Qloo cues predict attendance, or that they are more accurate than co-listening where both exist. See [evaluation](EVALUATION.md).
